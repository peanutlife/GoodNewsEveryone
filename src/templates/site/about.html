About Peanutlife

Peanutlife publishes ten true stories a day about things that got better, and then it stops. No infinite scroll, no notifications, no streaks. If you read all ten you are finished for the day, which is the whole idea.

How a day is made

The site is built by a program that runs every morning. There is no newsroom and no human editor between the publisher and you. In order:

  1. It fetches about 160 news feeds from the US, UK, Europe, East Asia, South Asia and Africa.
  2. A keyword filter drops the obvious non-candidates.
  3. An AI model vets what is left from the headline and opening lines, and marks each one yes, maybe or no.
  4. For the survivors, the model reads the publisher's own summary and decides whether it really is good news, which section it belongs in, and how strong a story it is. It writes the headline we show, the "in short" bullets, the "why it's good news" line and the longer significance note.
  5. The ten are then chosen arithmetically: highest-scoring stories first, with a cap of two per publisher and three per section so one source or one subject can't take over the day.
  6. A second AI model writes the daily letter and the section introductions from the list of ten.
  7. The morning email is built from the same published edition, so it always matches the front page.

So: the stories are real and reported by someone else; the summaries, the letter and the "why" are written by AI from that reporting; the selection is done by score and quota. Every story carries the publisher's name and a link, and we never rewrite their article as our own.

We check the output and change the rules and prompts when they get something wrong, but we do not read and approve each story before it is published. That is why every story page has a "Not good news? Tell us" link. We read those.

What we leave out

A story counts for us if something got better, was repaired, protected, discovered or done well, and it is true. Product launches, celebrity news, business results, listicles and anything whose main event is a death, crime, disaster or war are left out, even with a hopeful ending. Politics appears only when it works: a law passed, a service delivered, a ruling that extends rights. Never campaigns, polls or who is winning.

Our reader promise has the longer version, including how the page behaves and how ads are placed.

How it pays for itself

Ads, through Google AdSense. They are labelled, never placed above a headline and never shaped like a story. We take no money from publishers for linking to them and we don't run sponsored stories. Nothing in the ten is there because someone paid for it.

Getting in touch

Corrections, takedown requests, press, or a source we should be reading: contact@peanutlife.com. For what we store about you and how to have it deleted, see Privacy.
