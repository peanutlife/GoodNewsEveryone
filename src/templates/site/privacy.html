This page describes what the site actually does. If you find something here that does not match what you experience, write to contact@peanutlife.com and we will fix the page or the code.

Reading the site without an account

You can read every story without giving us anything. When you do, we set a cookie called pv containing a random id with no name, email or IP address in it. We use it for three things:

  • Counting one reader per day, so the number on the front page is real. We store the date and the random id, nothing else.
  • Remembering which stories you tapped ♥ on, so the hearts stay filled in on this device.
  • Keeping your answer to "how do you feel after today's ten" to one per day. We store the date, the random id, the answer and which edition you read.

Two more cookies store only a preference: edition (Global, US or East Asia) and mood (the order the ten are shown in). All three last up to a year and you can delete them in your browser at any time.

We do not log your IP address and we do not fingerprint your browser. Our hosting provider and CDN see your IP address in order to deliver the page, as any web host does. We read a country code from the CDN (CF-IPCountry) to guess your edition; we use it for that request and do not store it.

"One good thing from your own day" is kept in your browser's local storage. It never reaches our server unless you are signed in (see below). Your seven-day feelings trail and your place in today's ten are also local to your browser.

The morning email

If you subscribe, we store your email address, the date you subscribed, whether the subscription is active, a random unsubscribe token, when we last sent to you and how many emails we have sent. We use it only to send the daily digest. Every email has a one-tap unsubscribe link, which sets your subscription to inactive. We do not put tracking pixels in the email and we do not sell or rent the list.

If you give us your email to follow one specific story, we store that address, the story id, the date, and a token so you can unfollow. We use it only to tell you when that story has a next chapter.

Signing in

Accounts are passwordless. You type your email address, we email you a signed link that works for thirty minutes, and opening it signs you in. We store your email address, a generated username, when the account was created and when you last signed in. (A password column exists in the database for historical reasons; we fill it with a random value that is never used and never shown.)

Signing in sets a session cookie, and a "remember me" cookie that lasts ninety days.

Once you are signed in, these are stored against your account instead of only against the random cookie id: the stories you ♥ and when you last looked at your page, your daily feeling, and any "one good thing" notes you choose to save. Only you can read your notes through the site.

"Ask this story"

The question you type on a story page is sent to our AI provider together with that story's title, publisher, link, the publisher's own summary and the summary we wrote, and the answer comes straight back to your browser. We do not save your question or the answer in our database, and we do not attach your cookie id or account to it. Our provider processes it under their own terms in order to produce the answer. Don't type anything into that box you would not want sent to a third party.

Ads and analytics

Two Google services run on every page and both set their own cookies, which we cannot read:

  • Google AdSense (publisher ca-pub-4295146235414490) serves the ads that pay for the site. Google may use cookies and device identifiers to choose and measure ads. You can review and change what Google uses at My Ad Center and Ad Settings.
  • Google Analytics 4 (property G-V4SHHF8RVX) tells us how many people read which pages. We also send GA4 an event when you tap ♥ or open a publisher's link; those events carry the story id, never your email address or your notes.

Pages also load fonts from fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com, which means Google receives your IP address and browser details in order to serve the font files.

Google's own explanation of the data it handles for these services is at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Links to other sites

Every story links to the publisher that reported it, and some stories link to an organisation you could help. Those sites are not ours. Once you follow a link, that site's own privacy policy applies, and it can set its own cookies. We get no money from publishers for sending you to them.

Children

The kids section exists so stories can be read aloud to a child. The site is not aimed at children and we do not ask anyone's age. Please do not create an account or subscribe on a child's behalf.

How long we keep things, and how to get rid of them

  • Unsubscribe: the one-tap link at the bottom of any email, which stops the emails immediately.
  • Delete everything: email contact@peanutlife.com from the address you used, say you want your data deleted, and we will delete your subscriber record, your account, your ♥s, your feelings and your notes. We aim to do it within thirty days.
  • A copy of your data: ask at the same address and we will send you what we hold.
  • Cookies: clearing cookies for this site removes the random id, so the daily reader count, the hearts on that device and that device's feelings trail start again from nothing.

Daily reader counts and feeling tallies are kept as aggregate rows keyed by the random cookie id, with no way for us to connect them to a person unless that person signs in.

Where the data lives

The site runs on Render behind Cloudflare, with a PostgreSQL database. Email is sent over SMTP by our mail provider. Those providers process data on our behalf in order to run the site.
